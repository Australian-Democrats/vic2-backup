Item 03 / 04 — Cost of living

One bill, broken into the parts you are currently not shown.

  • Standardised breakdowns
  • Priced quarterly, not yearly
  • Essentials: >10% disclosed

Every bill, itemised.

Show what sits inside the price.

Energy retailers would have to show what sits inside the price — wholesale cost, network charges, environmental cost, margin. Insurers would have to show claims, reinsurance, admin and profit. Major supermarkets would have to disclose any rise over 10% on essential goods within six months, and energy prices would be reviewed quarterly instead of once a year. You can’t fix what you’re not allowed to see.

Item 04 / 04 — Housing

Three bars on one scale of homes: the 16,000 social homes the Government has committed this decade, the 80,000 the sector says are needed, and the nearly 100,000 homes in metropolitan Melbourne that were empty or underused in 2023.

  • More than double the waiting list
  • 30,660 Victorians homeless
  • VCOSS: 7,990 homes a year

Put homes first, not speculation.

Three numbers we want argued out in public.

These are directions we’re putting up for debate, not promises we could pass alone. The sector puts the need at 80,000 social homes this decade; the Government has committed 16,000 — while nearly 100,000 homes in metropolitan Melbourne sat empty or underused in 2023, more than double the number of households waiting for social housing. As of 2021, over 80% of low-income private renters were in rental stress, and Victoria’s homeless population is estimated at 30,660 against the 7,990 homes a year VCOSS says are needed. So we’d argue in public for housing as a human right in law, tax reform that discourages land banking and speculation, new social homes alongside the refurbishment of existing public housing towers, and Victorians actually asked what kind of homes they want.
